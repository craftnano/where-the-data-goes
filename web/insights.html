Six findings from the sub-processor graph

The map renders every company that publishes a sub-processor list and every vendor each company names. These are six patterns that showed up when the resulting dataset was counted.

01 Anthropic vs OpenAI #

4 shared vendors

Anthropic and OpenAI share only four sub-processors, and none of them are AI companies.

Anthropic lists 22 third parties on its public sub-processor page. OpenAI lists 16. The overlap is exactly four companies: Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Cloudflare. Each of the other 18 Anthropic entries and 12 OpenAI entries is specific to one lab.

02 Cloudflare's AI Gateway #

4 mutual-dependency loops

Cloudflare uses four major LLM providers, and all four of them list Cloudflare in return.

Cloudflare's published sub-processor list names OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and Groq as vendors for its AI Gateway product. Each of those four labs separately lists Cloudflare on their own sub-processor pages for CDN or edge security. That produces four pairs of mutual-dependency edges in the dataset. No single hyperscaler creates as many closed loops with the frontier labs.

04 Multi-model hedging #

34% vs 1%

A third of Cloud 100 seeds list both OpenAI and Anthropic. One S&P 100 seed does.

Across the full dataset, 67 companies name both OpenAI and Anthropic on their published sub-processor list. The breakdown by cohort is sharp. In the Forbes Cloud 100 the rate is 34%. In the S&P 100 it is 1%. The Nasdaq 100 is at 3%.

The dataset only reflects what companies disclose publicly, so this understates actual usage. The gap between cohorts is still large enough to be visible even with the under-reporting.

05 The quiet international AI stack #

7 US creative-AI products

ByteDance, Kuaishou, Alibaba, and a Saudi state-owned AI company appear on US sub-processor lists.

The dataset contains ten disclosed instances of sub-processors headquartered in China or operated by sovereign-state entities. BytePlus (the enterprise brand of ByteDance) provides the Seedance video-generation model to Adobe, Runway, Synthesia, and Figma. Kuaishou's Kling model is listed by Adobe. Humain, a Saudi state-owned AI company, is also listed by Adobe for image generation. Alibaba Cloud is listed by Fireworks AI and Zscaler. ByteDance appears on ElevenLabs' list.

These are self-disclosures by the US companies that use them. The dataset does not reach into the Chinese or Saudi companies' own vendor chains.

06 AWS at two hops #

98% reach AWS in ≤2 hops

Of the companies that publish a sub-processor list, 98% reach AWS in two hops or fewer.

Of the 164 publishing companies in the dataset, 138 name Amazon Web Services directly as a sub-processor. Another 23 reach AWS through a single intermediary, typically Snowflake, MongoDB, Datadog, or another hyperscaler-adjacent SaaS. The remaining three seeds either do not reach AWS at the depth the dataset covers, or do not list a cloud infrastructure vendor at all.

This measures disclosed vendor-chain paths, not actual data flows or storage locations.

About these numbers. These findings are counts derived from the sub-processor dataset collected in October 2026. The dataset covers roughly 320 unique seeds across the Nasdaq 100, S&P 100, Forbes Cloud 100 (2025), and a hand-curated set of additional SaaS, AI, backbone, and healthcare companies, plus manual observations for companies whose lists are not accessible by crawl.

Point-in-time. Sub-processor lists change frequently. Numbers above reflect the dataset at the moment of extraction and may be stale by the time you read them. Follow the pipeline on GitHub for the current graph.

Scope. These findings describe sub-processor relationships only. They do not describe infrastructure, every vendor relationship, breach surface, or security posture. A company's full exposure includes many relationships that are not publicly disclosed and do not appear in this dataset. See the main site's methodology for the full scope and limitations.

Reproducibility. Every number here can be re-derived from the committed data. Run scripts/report.py and scripts/export_json.py against the run IDs in data/runs/.
